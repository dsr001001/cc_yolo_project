# Barclaycard brand tracking across the three runs (11 October 2026)

Scope: every mention of Barclaycard or Barclays in the 11am (turn 1 only, Barclaycard was the source card so excluded as a target), 12pm (single pick, three turns) and 1230 (three ranked picks with per-fact sources, three turns) runs. Four models, two credit branches each. Nothing here is verified against Barclays' live pages; it records what the models said, where they got it, and why Barclaycard rose or fell.

## 3. What pushed Barclaycard up

- **The 36-month term with a near-3% fee fits the persona's "safety net" wish.** Fable A at 12pm: "36 months is a huge safety net, the fee is right at your limit, no conflicts, Barclaycard's eligibility checker is a proper soft-search tool, generally one of the more accepting of the big issuers". The "more accepting" claim was unsourced.
- **MSE's "definite 36 months" label** (24 Feb 2026 roundup) was the deciding factor for Fable B in both the 12pm and 1230 runs: certainty of term is valued highly for a Fair-credit applicant.
- **The tiered "up to" structure was read as a feature for Fair credit** by Fable B at 12pm: "applicants with Fair credit are typically offered a shorter term instead of being declined outright".
- **The 22-month 1.49% product won outright the moment it was seen.** Only Fable B surfaced it, from a product-page excerpt, and immediately ranked it first: half the fee of the 36-month cards with enough term for the plan.
- **Which? Best Buy and Recommended Provider status** on the no-fee card was cited by Fable A and Opus B as a quality signal.

## 4. What pushed Barclaycard down

- **The persona's budget killed the no-fee card.** 150 to 200 a month cannot clear 3,000 in 12 to 14 months (needs 215 to 250). Every model that led with the no-fee card at turn 1 dropped it at turn 2.
- **3.15% reads as "over 3%" to some models.** Haiku A at 12pm and 1230 excluded the 36m card for that reason alone. Opus, Fable and Sonnet treated 3.09% to 3.15% as acceptable. HSBC's 3.09% beat Barclaycard's 3.15% on fee in four runs for a difference of about 2 pounds.
- **Conflicting fee figures made Sonnet distrust it.** Sonnet A at 1230 found 3.45% and 2.49% on two versions of the same Money to the Masses page and ranked Barclaycard second "only because the figures conflict", then demoted it to unconfirmed when it could not reach the Barclaycard page.
- **"Up to" tiering versus "definite".** Where a model trusted the MSE guide's "36, 32 or 18" wording rather than the roundup's "definite", it preferred HSBC or Virgin Money, which MSE labels definite.
- **Page availability.** The URL variant barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer returned 404 for Sonnet A. Opus B tried two barclaycard.co.uk pages, both 404, and fell back to barclays.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards, which "didn't list age, income or residency rules". Fable B reported the search tool flagging Barclaycard's card listings as about six months old. By contrast hsbc.co.uk loaded for every model that tried it, and HSBC ended first in most runs.
- **Absence from the Which? 0% table as a long card.** In every version of the Which? table the models saw, Barclaycard appeared only as the 12-month no-fee card. Models that shortlist from Which? (Sonnet, Haiku B) therefore never considered the 36m card as a long-term option.
- **The cashback offer's conditions were read as a restriction.** "Needs a Barclays current account and the Barclays app" was quoted, and the persona had neither.

## 6. Actions this points to for Barclays

1. **Make every plausible product URL resolve.** Two models hit 404s on barclaycard.co.uk variants and one on the bare balance-transfer path. A 404 is the single event most likely to turn a top-three placing into "unconfirmed". Redirect legacy and shortened paths to the live product pages.
2. **Put eligibility criteria on the page the models land on.** barclays.co.uk's listing lacks age, income and residency rules; HSBC's and Santander's pages carry them and were quoted verbatim. Fable's "one of the more accepting issuers" claim was unsourced and would be stronger if a page said it.
3. **Decide and state whether 36 months is definite or tiered.** MSE's roundup says definite, MSE's guide says 36, 32 or 18. Models that saw "definite" picked Barclaycard; models that saw the tiers picked HSBC or Virgin. Whichever is true, say it on the product page and get MSE's guide aligned.
4. **Get the 36-month card into the Which? 0% table.** Sonnet and Haiku B shortlist from Which?, where Barclaycard shows only as a 12-month no-fee card. That is why Sonnet never led with Barclaycard in any run.
5. **Surface the 22-month 1.49% card in the comparison tables.** It won first place the only time a model saw it, and it fits the common "clear it in about two years" persona better than either headline product. It currently appears only as a product-page excerpt.
6. **Fee positioning.** 3.15% lost to HSBC's 3.09% in four runs on a 2 pound difference, and was excluded outright by Haiku as "over 3%". A fee at or under 3.00% passes the round-number filter the models apply.
7. **Clean up stale third-party figures.** Money to the Masses holds 3.45% and 2.49% variants; Finder's review is from April 2025 with a 15-month name and a 20,000 income rule. Models flag these conflicts and tell users to distrust the card.
8. **Freshness signals on the product pages.** One model's search tool labelled the Barclaycard listings as six months old. A visible "rates correct as of" date and structured data would help.
9. **Cashback conditions.** The current-account and app requirement was quoted as a reason the offer did not apply. If the cashback is meant to win new-to-Barclays customers, the conditions work against that in AI answers.
