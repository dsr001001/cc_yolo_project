## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

## Turn 1 (model)
Good move. At 24.9% you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest on £3,000, so switching to a 0% card will help a lot.

**The right card depends mostly on how fast you can pay it off.** There are two kinds of deal:

**1. Long 0% period with a transfer fee (best if you need time)**
The longest deals I found in recent comparison tables (early September 2026) were:
- **TSB Platinum Balance Transfer:** up to 38 months at 0%, 3.49% fee (about £105 on £3,000)
- **HSBC, Virgin Money, NatWest:** around 36 months at 0%, about 3.1% fee (about £93)
- **Vanquis Low APR:** 36 months with no transfer fee. That's unusual, so read the terms closely. Vanquis mainly serves people with weaker credit, and its rate after the 0% period is likely to be high.

To clear £3,000 within 36 months you'd pay about £86 a month.

**2. Short 0% period with no fee (best if you can pay it off quickly)**
- **Barclaycard** has offered 12 to 14 months at 0% with no transfer fee.

That means about £215 to £250 a month, but you'd pay nothing extra at all.

A rough rule: if you can comfortably pay £220 or more a month, go fee-free. If you can't, take a longer deal and accept the fee. Either way you'll pay far less than you are now.

One caution: these offers change often, and the sources I found didn't all agree on lengths. Please check the live tables on MoneySavingExpert or Which? before you apply.

**Tips that matter more than which card you pick:**
- **Use an eligibility checker first** (MSE, Which?, or the lender's own). It does a "soft search" that doesn't affect your credit score. You might be offered a shorter 0% period or a lower limit than advertised, and the checker will show this.
- **You usually can't transfer within the same banking group.** For example, a Lloyds card can't go to Halifax or MBNA.
- **Make the transfer within the deadline**, often 60 to 90 days after opening. Miss it and you won't get the 0% rate.
- **Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum.** One missed payment can cancel the 0% deal.
- **Don't spend on the new card.** New purchases often aren't at 0%.
- **Your limit might be under £3,000.** If so, move what you can and pay off the rest from the old card first.
- **Have a plan for the end date.** Once the 0% ends, the rate is often 25% or more.

**To narrow it down to one card, could you tell me:**
1. How much could you realistically pay each month? a) Under £100 b) £100 to £200 c) £200 to £300 d) More than £300
2. Which bank is your current card with? a) Lloyds / Halifax / Bank of Scotland / MBNA b) Barclaycard c) HSBC / First Direct / M&S d) NatWest / RBS e) Other (please say which)
3. How's your credit history? a) Good, no missed payments b) A few late payments in the past c) Not sure

## Turn 2 (model)
You have a clean credit record, £150 to £200 a month to pay, and no ties to any big bank. For you I'd apply for **HSBC's 36-month 0% balance transfer card**. Run the eligibility checker before you apply.

**Why HSBC:**
- **The 0% lasts 36 months.** You plan to clear it in 18 to 24 months, so you'd have a buffer of a year or more.
- **The fee is about 3.09%, about £93 on £3,000.** That's the lowest fee I found among the long deals and right at your limit. It's a tiny fraction of the roughly £60 a month you're paying in interest now.
- **You can't be blocked for already banking with HSBC.** Your current card is from a small lender, so HSBC will let you transfer it.

**Close alternatives if HSBC turns you down or offers less than 36 months:**
- **Virgin Money or NatWest:** both offer about 36 months with a fee of about 3.1%. They're practically the same deal, so choose whichever eligibility checker gives you better odds.
- **TSB Platinum:** 38 months with a 3.49% fee, about £105. It's slightly above your limit and only about £12 more, but you don't need the extra 2 months.
- **Nationwide (30 months, 2.99% fee):** you'd need to be a Nationwide member, so this probably isn't open to you.

**How it works out:**
Fee included, you'd owe about £3,093. That's about £129 a month to clear it in 24 months, or about £155 to clear it in 20. Both fit your budget, and the 36-month window covers you if things get tight.

**Steps:**
1. **Check eligibility with a soft search first.** Use HSBC's own checker or the one on MSE. It won't affect your credit score. Apply only if it shows a high chance of acceptance at the full 36 months.
2. **Make one application at a time.** Several hard searches close together can hurt your chances.
3. **Check the credit limit you're given.** If it's under £3,000, transfer what you can and keep paying the rest off the old card.
4. **Make the transfer within the deadline.** It's usually 60 to 90 days after opening, and you'll find it in the terms.
5. **Set up a direct debit for a fixed £150 a month, not the minimum payment.** Never missing a payment keeps the 0% safe, and the fixed amount means you'll clear it on time.
6. **Close the old card once its balance is zero,** or keep it unused if you'd like it for your credit history.

Offers change fast, so check HSBC's page for the current length and fee before applying. The sources I found disagreed a little on the exact terms.
