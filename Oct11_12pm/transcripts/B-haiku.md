## Turn 1 (model)
The right card depends mainly on how much you can pay each month. If you can clear the £3,000 within about a year, a no-fee card is cheapest. If you need longer, a card with a small fee still usually beats paying 24.9% interest, because the fee is a one-off cost of roughly £40 to £105 on £3,000.

Current UK options, based on Which? and MoneySavingExpert listings from early October 2026:

- **No fee, up to 12 months at 0%:** Barclaycard Platinum (12 months) and Santander Everyday No Balance Transfer Fee (12 months). NatWest and RBS are 11 months.
- **Small fee, about 20 to 24 months at 0%:** Tesco Bank (20 months, 1.25% fee, about £38 on £3,000) and Virgin Money (24 months, 1.95% fee, about £59).
- **Higher fee, about 35 to 38 months at 0%:** Halifax and Lloyds (35 months, 2.49% fee, about £75), Capital One (36 months, 2.9% fee, about £87), HSBC (36 months, 3.09% fee, about £93) and TSB (38 months, 3.49% fee, about £105).

Rough monthly payments to clear £3,000 inside the 0% period:
- No fee, 12 months: about £250 a month.
- Tesco, 20 months: about £152 a month, with the fee added to the balance.
- HSBC, 36 months: about £86 a month, with the fee added to the balance.

Points to keep in mind:
- The new card's limit may be lower than £3,000, and the headline offer depends on your credit profile. Eligibility checkers on MoneySavingExpert or Moneyfacts usually run a soft check that doesn't affect your score.
- Keep paying at least the minimum on your current card until the transfer shows as complete. Transfers can take a few days to a few weeks.
- Avoid new spending on the transfer card. Purchases are often not at 0%, and paying the balance down can be slower.
- Once the 0% period ends, most of these cards move to about 24.9%, so aim to clear the balance before then.
- Figures can change and sites don't always agree, so check the provider's current terms before you apply.

Thanks. If I get declined for that one, what's my next best option? And is there anything that could trip me up along the way?

## Turn 3 (model)
If Virgin Money declines you, wait a few weeks before applying again, and check the first option with its eligibility checker rather than a full application. Every full application leaves a hard search on your credit file, and several in a short period can lower your score further. Use the checker on the next card too, so you apply only where the checker suggests you're likely to be accepted.

Next best options, in order:
1. **Capital One (36 months at 0%, 2.9% fee, about £87):** the longest 0% period under your 3% limit, and it fits your budget with room to spare. Its approval odds for a Fair credit band with no existing relationship are uncertain, so run the checker first.
2. **Tesco Bank (20 months at 0%, 1.25% fee, about £38):** the cheapest fee, and 20 months still covers your £150 to £200 monthly plan. The margin is smaller, so only apply if you can pay at least about £153 a month.
3. **Halifax or Lloyds (35 months at 0%, 2.49% fee, about £75):** the fee and length are strong, but these are high-street banks where you have no relationship. The checker will tell you whether it's worth applying.

If all of these decline you, a personal loan to repay the card can work, and a free debt adviser can review your options. StepChange and Citizens Advice both offer free help, and MoneyHelper is the government's guidance service.

Things that could trip you up:
- **Transfer deadlines:** many offers only give the 0% rate if the transfer is made within a set time after opening the account. Check the deadline in the terms.
- **Transfer limit:** the card may approve less than £3,000. You'd need to keep the rest on your current card, which keeps paying 24.9%.
- **Missed payments:** a missed or late payment on the new card can cancel the 0% deal and bring the rate up to the standard APR. Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum, and add a monthly reminder for the full payment.
- **Spending on the card:** new purchases usually don't get 0%. Most card providers apply your payments to the 0% balance first, but check your terms. Don't use the card for new spending.
- **Your current card:** keep paying at least the minimum until the transfer shows as complete, and check the statement afterwards. Don't close it straight away, since closing a card can reduce your available credit and affect your score.
- **After the 0% period:** the rate usually rises to about 24.9%, so any balance left at that point starts costing interest again.
- **Credit file:** check your Experian, Equifax and TransUnion reports for errors before applying. Being on the electoral roll helps, and it's worth confirming that it's up to date.
