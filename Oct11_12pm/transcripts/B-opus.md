The right card mostly depends on **how fast you can pay it off**. With balance transfers, a longer 0% period usually means a bigger one-off fee. I checked the comparison sites, and these are the main options right now. They change often, so check the terms before you apply.

**Longest 0% periods (fee about 3 to 3.5%, so roughly £93 to £105 on £3,000)**

**No-fee, shorter deals (best if you can clear it fast)**
- **Barclaycard** and **Virgin Money** both have no-fee options at around 12 months at 0%. Over 12 months that's £250 a month, with no fee at all.

**Rough rule of thumb**
- If you can pay **£250+ a month**, a 12-month no-fee card is cheapest.
- If you can manage about **£85 to £90 a month**, a 36-month card with a roughly 3% fee gives you time to clear it.
- Anything in between: pick the cheapest fee with a 0% period that's long enough to clear it, plus a few months to spare.

**Before you apply**
1. **Use an eligibility checker first** (MoneySavingExpert, Which?, or the card company's own). These use a "soft search", which doesn't affect your credit score, and show your chances of being accepted. Each full application leaves a mark on your credit file.
2. **You usually can't transfer between cards from the same banking group**, for example from one Lloyds group card to another.
3. **Do the transfer within the deadline**, often 60 to 90 days, or the 0% rate won't apply.
4. **Set up a direct debit and never miss a payment.** A missed payment can end the 0% deal.
5. **Don't spend on the new card.** Purchases often have a different, higher rate.
6. Plan to pay it off before the 0% ends, because after that it jumps to a normal APR, often 24%+.
