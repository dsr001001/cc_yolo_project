## Turn 2 (model)
The best fit for what you've described is the **Nationwide balance transfer credit card**: 0% on transfers for 30 months, with a 2.99% fee (about £90 on £3,000).

Why it fits:
- At £150 a month, the £3,090 total clears in about 21 months. At £175 it clears in about 18 months. Either way you're inside your 18 to 24 month window.
- The 30-month 0% period gives you a safety net of about nine months beyond that.
- The 2.99% fee is within your limit of about 3%, and it's the lowest fee of the longer 0% cards I found.

The catch: Nationwide requires an existing Nationwide current account, savings account or mortgage to apply. Since your current account is with another building society, you'd need to open a Nationwide savings account first. That's usually a quick online process, but it is a step you'd need to take before applying.

Two things to watch:
- The 0% rate only applies to transfers made within 90 days of opening the account, so make the transfer as soon as the card is approved. Later transfers carry a higher fee.
- Pay off the balance before the 30 months end. Any amount left after that moves to the standard variable rate, which is 24.9%.

If you'd rather not open a Nationwide account, the next best option is HSBC's 36-month balance transfer card, with a 3.09% fee. I didn't confirm whether HSBC accepts applicants with no existing HSBC relationship, so check that before relying on it. Confirm current terms on the Nationwide website before you apply.

Sources: Nationwide (balance transfer credit card; credit card rates and details; 30-month 0% announcement, January 2026), Money to the Masses (0% balance transfer deals).
