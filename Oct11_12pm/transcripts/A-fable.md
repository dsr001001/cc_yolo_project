# Run A-fable (Oct11_12pm)

## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

## Turn 1 (model)
Good move: at 24.9% on £3,000 you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest alone, so a 0% card should save you several hundred pounds.

**Quick snapshot of the UK market right now** (comparison tables dated late August/early September 2026, so double-check before applying):

- **Longest 0% periods:** TSB Platinum at 38 months (3.49% fee), with Vanquis, HSBC, Virgin Money, NatWest and Barclaycard at around 36 months (fees roughly 3.1 to 3.5%, though Which? lists Vanquis at 36 months fee-free; worth verifying as that would be unusually generous).
- **Fee-free options:** Barclaycard at 14 months with no fee, Virgin Money and Santander at 12 months (Santander's has a £24 annual fee).

**Which is right for you depends on how fast you can clear it.** On £3,000:
- Pay £215+/month: you'd clear it in 14 months, so a **fee-free card** (e.g. Barclaycard 14 months) costs you £0 in fees.
- Pay around £100/month: you need about 30 months, so a **long 0% card** makes sense; a ~3.1% fee is around £93, still far cheaper than the interest you're paying now.
- Somewhere in between (~£150/month): a mid-length card with a lower fee (e.g. Lloyds ~33 months at ~2.49%, or Nationwide 30 months at 2.99%) could be the sweet spot.

A couple of things before I point you at one specific card:

**1. Roughly how much can you put towards it each month?** a) £100 or less b) £100 to £150 c) £150 to £250 d) £250+
**2. Who is your current card with?** (You can't balance-transfer within the same banking group, e.g. Lloyds / Halifax / Bank of Scotland / MBNA, or NatWest / RBS, or Barclays / Barclaycard.) a) Barclaycard b) Lloyds / Halifax / MBNA c) NatWest / RBS d) HSBC / First Direct / M&S e) Other
**3. How's your credit record?** a) Good/excellent, never missed payments b) Fair, a missed payment or two in the last few years c) Not sure

Whatever you pick, use an eligibility checker first (MoneySavingExpert, ClearScore and the banks' own sites all have one); it won't dent your credit file, and the long-0% cards are the fussiest about who they accept. Also note the headline 0% length is only guaranteed to those with the best credit; others may be offered a shorter term. Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum and don't spend on the new card.

## Turn 2 (model)
Thanks: with £150 to 200 a month, a clean credit record, no ties to any of the big issuers and a preference for a long buffer at a fee of around 3%, that narrows it down nicely.

**My pick: the Barclaycard Platinum 36-month balance transfer card** (fee around 3.15%, so roughly £95 on £3,000).

Why that one:
- **36 months is a huge safety net.** At £150/month you'd clear £3,095 (balance plus fee) in about 21 months; at £200 it's 16 months. Even if life gets in the way for a few months, you're nowhere near the end of the 0% period.
- **The fee is right at your limit.** It's a few pounds over a strict 3%, but at this balance the difference between 3.1% and 3.15% is about £2. Not worth fussing over.
- **No conflicts.** Your current card isn't Barclays/Barclaycard, so you can transfer to it.
- **Barclaycard's eligibility checker** is a proper soft-search tool that tells you your chances without touching your credit file, and they're generally one of the more accepting of the big issuers for people with a clean record.

**Near-identical alternatives** if the checker gives Barclaycard a lukewarm result: HSBC and NatWest both do 36 months at about 3.1% fee. The three are essentially interchangeable, so take whichever the eligibility checkers rate you highest for. (TSB's 38-month card is the longest on the market but its 3.49% fee breaches your ceiling for two months you won't use.)

Two cards I'd steer you away from despite them looking tempting: **Nationwide's 30-month/2.99% card** requires an existing Nationwide account, and **Virgin Money's** card is now part of the Nationwide group too. And the old Lloyds 33-month deal appears to have shrunk to around 23 months.

**One alternative worth knowing about:** if you'd rather save ~£25 to 30 and are confident in the £150+, a 24 to 26 month card with a ~2% fee exists in most issuers' ranges. But given you've asked for a safety net, I'd stick with the 36-month option; the extra buffer costs you less than a month of your current interest.

**Practical steps:**
1. Run eligibility checkers on MoneySavingExpert's Credit Club or ClearScore first (one search, several cards, no credit file impact).
2. Apply for the one with the best odds. Check the credit limit they give you; if it's under £3,100, transfer what you can and clear the rest on the old card first.
3. Transfer within the first 60 days (Barclaycard's deadline; it's 90 for some others) or you lose the promo fee.
4. Set up a direct debit of at least the minimum, then fix your own payment at £150+, and don't spend on the new card.
5. Once the old card is at zero, consider closing it or at least cutting it up.

## Turn 3 (user)
Thanks. If I get declined for that one, what's my next best option? And is there anything that could trip me up along the way?

## Turn 3 (model)
**If Barclaycard says no**

Your next best option is **HSBC's 36-month balance transfer card** (fee about 3.1%), followed by **NatWest's 36-month card** (also about 3.1%). They're the same deal in all but name, so just move down the list. If all three long cards turn you down, drop to the next tier: something in the 24 to 30 month range from the likes of Tesco Bank, M&S Bank, Halifax or MBNA, which is still plenty for your timeline and often easier to get.

But a few things about the "declined" scenario itself:

- **Don't apply blind.** Every full application leaves a hard search on your credit file, and two or three in quick succession make lenders nervous. Run the soft-search eligibility checkers first (MoneySavingExpert's Credit Club or ClearScore cover most issuers in one go, or use Barclaycard's own). Only apply where you're shown 70%+ odds. With a clean record and no missed payments, you should get a clear picture before committing.
- **If you are declined, wait.** Give it a few weeks before trying the next card, and in the meantime check your credit reports (Experian, Equifax and TransUnion, all free) for anything odd: an old address, a missing electoral roll entry, a mis-recorded late payment. A small online lender card may also report differently from a high-street one, so check it's showing correctly.
- **A "yes, but" is more likely than a "no".** The commoner outcome for someone in your position is approval at a shorter 0% period (say 24 to 30 months instead of 36) or a credit limit below £3,000. Both are still fine for your plan (24 months at £150/month works), so don't reject it reflexively.

**Things that could trip you up**
1. **Missing the transfer deadline.** Barclaycard gives 60 days, most others 90. After that the fee jumps (often to 5%) or the 0% doesn't apply. Set up the transfer as soon as the card arrives.
2. **A credit limit below your balance.** Transfer what you can; keep paying the remainder on the old card hard until it's gone. Don't leave the leftover drifting at 24.9%.
3. **Missing a single payment.** Most issuers withdraw the 0% deal entirely for one missed payment. A direct debit for the minimum is your insurance; then pay your £150 to 200 on top manually or set the direct debit to a fixed amount.
4. **Spending on the new card.** Purchases usually aren't at 0% and, worse, your payments may be allocated in a way that keeps the interest-bearing balance alive. Treat it as a repayment-only card.
5. **Spending on the old card.** Once it's cleared, the temptation is to treat the freed-up limit as spare money. Close it, or at least remove it from your phone and wallet.
6. **Not watching the end date.** At £150/month you'll be done around month 21, well before 36, but put the promo end date in your calendar anyway. If anything slips, you'd want to arrange another transfer a month or two before interest kicks in.
7. **Applying for other credit at the same time.** A new phone contract, car finance or a loan in the weeks around your application lowers your chances. Get the balance transfer sorted first.
8. **Fee maths.** Make sure you've budgeted for £3,000 plus the roughly £95 fee, so your monthly figure is based on £3,095, not £3,000.

One last reassurance: the fee is a one-off, so the worst case (paying ~£95 to borrow £3,000 for up to three years) is still less than two months of what you're paying now.
