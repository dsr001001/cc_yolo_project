## How the credit branch changed the answer

- **Fable** changed its pick's reasoning rather than the brand: Barclaycard in both branches, but in branch B it argued Barclaycard's tiered "up to" offer suits Fair credit, dropped HSBC, NatWest and TSB as good-credit-only, and made Vanquis the backup.
- **Haiku** changed brand: Nationwide (A) versus Virgin Money 24m (B), and in B it ranked a Fair-credit path (Capital One, Tesco) and named debt charities.
- **Sonnet** changed brand for a different reason: Lloyds (A) because it was the longest deal under 3%, versus HSBC (B) where it relied on the Which? table. Branch B added warnings about shorter offers and lower limits.
- **Opus** did not change brand: HSBC in both. Branch B stressed the "definite 36 months" over TSB's "up to", and added Tesco Bank and Vanquis as fair-credit fallbacks.

## Other observations

- **Question count at turn 1:** Haiku asked 1 question, Fable 1 or 3, Opus 3 or 4, Sonnet 4. Every model asked about monthly affordability. Opus, Sonnet and Fable also asked for the current issuer, so the "no major bank relationship" answer mattered.
- **The 3% fee ceiling drove the brand choice.** Three of the eight picks (Lloyds 2.49%, Nationwide 2.99%, Virgin 1.95%) exist only because those models honoured the ceiling strictly. The other five treated 3.09% to 3.15% as acceptable.
- **Source dependence.** Which? was cited in all 8 runs, MoneySavingExpert in 6. Runs that leaned on Which? tables converged on TSB, HSBC, Virgin, NatWest, Vanquis. Runs that searched provider pages or news pieces (Sonnet A, Haiku A) surfaced Lloyds and Nationwide instead. Less mainstream sources appeared too: Kaeltripton, Pocketwise, Gazette, checkmyfile.
- **Conflicting facts between models, unverified by me:** whether Nationwide requires membership; whether Virgin Money is now under Nationwide (Fable B said April 2026); whether the Vanquis Low APR card is 0% or 9.9% to 12.9%; Virgin Money's length and fee; Lloyds' length. These are worth checking before drawing conclusions about accuracy.
- **Data freshness.** Most models flagged that their tables were dated January to early October 2026 and told the user to check live terms. Sonnet A was the most cautious, refusing to recommend Virgin Money because it could not confirm the current offer.
- **Compared with the 11am run** (Barclaycard as the source card, Lloyds as the bank): removing those exclusions brought Barclaycard into the top picks (Fable, both branches) and let Lloyds and Nationwide appear, which they had not at 11am. TSB, HSBC, Virgin Money and NatWest were universal in both runs.
