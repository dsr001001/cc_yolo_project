# Run A-opus

## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

## Turn 1 (model)
Moving it to a 0% card is a good plan. At 24.9%, £3,000 is costing you roughly £60 a month in interest, so moving it will save you money. Which card is best mostly depends on **how fast you can pay it off**. So here's what's on offer now, and then a few quick questions.

**What's available (from comparison sites in September 2026, so check the latest terms before you apply):**

| Type | Example cards | 0% period | Transfer fee | Fee on £3,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Longest 0% | TSB Platinum Balance Transfer | up to 38 months | 3.49% | about £105 |
| Long 0% | HSBC, Virgin Money, NatWest | up to 36 months | about 3.1% | about £93 |
| No fee | Barclaycard Platinum No Fee, Virgin Money, Santander | about 12 to 14 months | 0% | £0 |

**How to choose:**
- **If you can pay about £215 to £250 a month**, a no-fee card with 12 to 14 months at 0% is cheapest. You pay nothing extra.
- **If it'll take longer**, go for a long 0% card. The fee is worth it: about £105 once, compared with roughly £60 a month in interest now. Over 38 months, £3,000 plus the fee works out to about £82 a month.
- The best fit is usually the shortest 0% period you can *definitely* clear it in. Leave yourself a few months of slack.

**Things to watch:**
- **Use eligibility checkers first.** Use the soft-search checkers on Which?, MoneySavingExpert, ClearScore or the card provider's own site. They don't affect your credit score. The 0% period you actually get offered can be shorter than the advertised one.
- **You usually can't transfer between cards from the same bank group.** For example, Lloyds, Halifax and Bank of Scotland are one group, and NatWest and RBS are another.
- **Make the transfer within the deadline.** It's often 60 to 90 days after opening the card, or the 0% offer and the low fee may not apply.
- **Never miss a minimum payment.** You can lose the 0% rate. Set up a direct debit for your monthly payment amount, not just the minimum.
- **Don't spend on the new card.** Spending usually isn't at 0%, and many of these cards charge a high rate after the offer ends (Vanquis is around 35.9%).

**To narrow it down to one card, could you tell me:**
1. Roughly how much can you pay off each month? a) Under £100 b) £100 to £200 c) £200 to £300 d) More than £300
2. How's your credit history? a) Good, no problems b) A few late payments c) Not sure
3. Which bank issues your current card? (So I can rule out cards you can't transfer to.)
4. Would you also use the new card for spending, or only for clearing this debt?
