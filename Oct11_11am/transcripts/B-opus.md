# Run B-opus

## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

## Turn 1 (model)
Moving it is a good idea. At 24.9% APR, £3,000 is costing you about £60 a month in interest, so a 0% deal can save you hundreds. Which card is best mostly comes down to **how much you can pay off each month**, because these cards trade a longer 0% period against a fee.

**The main options I found (from comparison tables dated August and September 2026, so check the current terms):**

| Card type | Example | 0% period | Transfer fee on £3,000 | Monthly payment to clear it in time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Longest deal | TSB Platinum Balance Transfer | Up to 38 months | 3.49%, about £105 | about £82 |
| Long deal, lower fee | HSBC / Virgin Money / NatWest | Up to 36 months | about 3.1%, about £93 | about £86 |
| Long deal, no fee | Vanquis Low APR Balance Transfer | 36 months | £0 | about £83 |
| Short deal, no fee | Barclaycard Platinum No Fee | About 12 months | £0 | £250 |

**How to choose:**
- **If you can pay about £250 a month or more,** take a no-fee card. You'd pay nothing extra at all.
- **If you can only pay around £80 to £150 a month,** take a 36 to 38 month card. The fee of about £100 is much less than the interest you're paying now.
- **About Vanquis:** having no fee and 36 months sounds ideal. But its rate after the 0% period (and on any spending) is 35.9%, so it only makes sense if you're certain you'll clear it in time and won't spend on it.

**Before you apply:**
1. **Use eligibility checkers first.** They're on the card issuers' sites, ClearScore and MoneySavingExpert. They do a soft search, which doesn't affect your credit score. The headline 0% period isn't guaranteed: some lenders give some applicants a shorter one.
2. **You usually can't transfer between banks in the same group** (for example, Lloyds, Halifax and MBNA are one group). Pick a card from a different group to your current one.
3. **Make the transfer within the deadline,** often 60 to 90 days from opening, or the 0% may not apply.
4. **Never miss a payment.** Set up a Direct Debit for at least the minimum. Missing one can end the 0% deal.
5. **Don't spend on the new card.** Purchases usually charge interest straight away.
6. **Divide the balance by the number of 0% months and pay that every month,** so it's all gone before the deal ends.

**To narrow it down to one card, could you tell me:**
1. Roughly how much could you pay off each month? a) Under £100 b) £100 to £200 c) £250 or more
2. How's your credit history? a) Good, no missed payments b) A few blips c) Not sure
3. Who is your current card with? (So I can rule out cards from the same banking group.)
4. Do you want to keep using a card for everyday spending, or just clear this debt? a) Just clear the debt b) I'd like to spend on it too
