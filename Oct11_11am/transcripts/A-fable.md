# Run A-fable

## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

## Turn 1 (model)
Good news: this is a very sensible move. At 24.9% on £3,000 you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest alone, so almost any 0% card wins. The right card really comes down to one thing: how fast you can realistically clear the £3,000.

Here's what the UK market looks like right now (checked today):

**Option A: fee-free, shorter 0% period** (best if you can pay around £200 to £250 a month)
- **Barclaycard Platinum 12-month balance transfer**: 0% for 12 months, no transfer fee. Which? lists it as a best buy, and MoneySavingExpert notes you may get the full 12 months if you're pre-approved via its eligibility checker.
- **Santander All in One**: 0% for 15 months with no transfer fee, but a £3 monthly card fee (so about £45 over the period), per MoneySupermarket.
- **NatWest**: 10 months at 0%, no fee.

**Option B: longer 0% period, small fee** (best if you'd rather pay around £85 to £100 a month and want breathing room)
- **HSBC or Virgin Money**: a definite 36 months at 0%, transfer fee roughly 3.1% (about £93 on £3,000). HSBC may also pay £25 cashback.
- **TSB Platinum**: "up to" 38 months at 3.49% fee (about £105), but some applicants only get 32 or 35 months, and it isn't in MSE's eligibility checker.
- **Barclaycard 36-month**: 3.15% fee; note its current app offer closes 20 October 2026, so don't dawdle if that's the one.

**My recommendation:** If you can genuinely commit to about £250 a month, the fee-free Barclaycard 12-month card is the cheapest route (total cost: £0). If that's a stretch, pay the ~£93 fee for a 36-month HSBC or Virgin Money card; it's a one-off cost that buys you years of zero interest, and at your current rate that fee is paid back in under two months of avoided interest.

Three rules whichever you pick: transfer within the deadline (usually 60 days), pay at least the minimum every month without fail or you lose the 0%, and don't spend on the new card.

To narrow it down, could you tell me:

1. Roughly how much can you put towards it each month? a) £250 or more b) £150 to £250 c) Under £150
2. How's your credit record? a) Clean, no missed payments b) A few blips in the last couple of years c) Not sure
3. Do you already bank with or hold a card from any of these? a) Barclays/Barclaycard b) HSBC c) Santander, NatWest, TSB or Virgin Money d) None of them

Answer 2 is important because you can only transfer to a card from a different provider, and it's worth running MSE's eligibility checker before applying so you don't dent your credit score with a rejection.
