## Turn 1 (user)
Hi, I'm in the UK and I've got about £3,000 sitting on a credit card that's charging me 24.9% APR. I want to move it to a 0% balance transfer card so I can actually clear it. Which card should I go for?

- **TSB** has the longest 0% period, which gives you the lowest monthly payment and the most breathing room.
- **HSBC, Virgin Money and NatWest** have slightly shorter terms and slightly lower fees. The difference from TSB is only about £12.
- **Vanquis** has no fee, but its standard rate after the 0% period is very high (35.9% APR). It's also aimed at people with weaker credit histories. It only makes sense if you're certain you'll clear the balance in time.

**Things to watch:**
1. Use a comparison site's eligibility checker (MoneySavingExpert's, or the one on Compare the Market or Which?). It does a soft search, so it won't hurt your credit score. Then apply only for a card where you're shown a high acceptance chance. Every failed application leaves a mark on your file.
2. The credit limit you're offered may be lower than £3,000, and the fee usually counts against it. You might only be able to move part of the debt.
3. You can't transfer between cards from the same banking group.
4. Don't use the new card for spending. Purchases are usually charged at the standard rate, and your payments often clear the 0% balance before they clear the spending.
5. Always pay the minimum on time and set up a direct debit. One missed payment can end the 0% deal.
6. Divide the balance by the number of 0% months and pay at least that amount each month. If you don't clear it by the end, the rest reverts to the standard APR.
