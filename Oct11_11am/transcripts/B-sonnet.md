## Turn 1 (model)
Good move. At 24.9% APR, £3,000 costs you roughly £60 a month in interest, so a 0% card will help a lot. I checked current UK offers, and these look best right now:

**What I'd do**
- **If you want the lowest total cost and can clear the debt within 36 months:** the no-fee Vanquis card saves you about £100. Its 35.9% APR only matters if you haven't cleared the balance by the end of the 0% period. Vanquis tends to accept people with weaker credit histories, but you'd be stuck with a nasty rate if you fall behind.
- **If you want the longest runway, or a safer rate after the 0% period:** the TSB card (38 months) or the HSBC or Virgin Money cards (36 months, about 3.1% fee) are the usual choices. Per MoneyWeek, the TSB card needs the transfer done within 90 days of opening, the minimum transfer is £100, and you can't move debt from another TSB card.
- **Check how long the transfer window lasts:** most cards only give you the 0% rate on transfers made within the first 60 to 90 days.

**Practical points**
- Clearing £3,000 plus a fee of about £100 over 36 months is roughly £86 a month, or about £82 a month over 38 months. Set up a direct debit for that amount rather than the minimum payment. A single missed payment can cost you the 0% rate.
- Don't spend on the new card. Purchases usually get no 0% rate, and payments often clear the 0% balance first. Hold the old card, but don't use it.
- You may be offered a lower credit limit than £3,000 plus the fee, so you might not be able to move everything.
- Use the lender's eligibility checker, or one on a comparison site, before you apply. These do a soft search that doesn't affect your credit file. Several hard applications in a row can hurt your score.
