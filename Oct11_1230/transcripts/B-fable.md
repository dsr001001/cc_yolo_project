## Turn 1 (model)
Good news: on £3,000 at 24.9% you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest, so a 0% card should save you several hundred pounds even after a transfer fee. Here are my top three, ranked, with the reasoning and a source link on every figure so you can check it. Everything below is from pages I found today, but deals change often and some are "up to" offers, so run [MoneySavingExpert's eligibility calculator](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/) before applying; MSE says it will tell you the 0% length you'd get before you apply.

**1. Barclaycard Platinum 36-month balance transfer (0% for 36 months, 3.15% fee)**
- MSE lists this as a *definite* 36 months at 0% with a 3.15% fee, meaning every accepted applicant gets the full period rather than a shorter backup deal ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026 roundup](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)).
- You must make the transfer within 60 days of opening the account to get the 0% deal ([Barclaycard balance transfer page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Why first: on £3,000 the fee is about £94.50, and 36 months means clearing it at roughly £83 a month. The "definite" length is the big plus; you know exactly what you're getting, which matters for a plan to actually clear the debt.

**2. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Mastercard (0% for up to 38 months, 3.49% fee)**
- TSB's own page says 0% for up to 38 months on balance transfers, with a 3.49% fee on transfers made in the first 90 days, and a representative APR of 24.9% (variable) afterwards ([TSB balance transfers page](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers.html)).
- MSE calls it the longest 0% deal on the market but flags that it's only "up to" 38 months, so some accepted applicants may be offered a shorter term ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)). Which? also lists it at 38 months with a 3.49% fee and 24.9% APR after ([Which? best 0% balance transfer cards](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).
- Why second: two extra months, but a higher fee (about £104.70 on £3,000) and no guarantee of the full length. Only worth picking over Barclaycard if the eligibility calculator says you'll get the full 38 months and you want the lowest possible monthly payment (about £79).

**3. Barclaycard Platinum 14-month no-fee balance transfer (0% for up to 14 months, 0% fee)**
- Barclaycard's page lists 0% on balance transfers for up to 14 months from account opening, 0% transfer fee, transfers must be made within 60 days, and 24.9% representative APR (variable) after the deal ([Barclaycard balance transfer page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). It's listed as an "up to" deal, so some applicants get less ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)); a Finder review notes a minimum income requirement of £20,000 a year ([Finder review](https://www.finder.com/uk/barclaycard-platinum-15-month-balance-transfer-visa)).
- Why third rather than first: it's the cheapest option (no fee at all), and MSE's rule of thumb is to always go no-fee if you're sure you can repay in time ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)). But clearing £3,000 in 14 months means about £215 a month, every month. If that's comfortable for you, this card moves to number one. If it isn't, paying a ~£95 fee for 36 months is the safer route; the whole point is to be certain you clear it before the interest returns.

A few things that apply to all of them: you generally can't transfer between cards from the same provider, so if your current card is a Barclaycard, options 1 and 3 are out ([MSE balance transfer guide](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum, don't spend on the new card, and ideally set a fixed payment that clears the balance before the 0% ends.
