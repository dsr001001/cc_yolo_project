## Turn 1 (model)
Good news: on £3,000 at 24.9% you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest, so a 0% card should save you several hundred pounds even after a transfer fee. Here are my top three, ranked, with the reasoning and a source link on every figure so you can check it. Everything below is from pages I found today, but deals change often and some are "up to" offers, so run [MoneySavingExpert's eligibility calculator](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/) before applying; MSE says it will tell you the 0% length you'd get before you apply.

**1. Barclaycard Platinum 36-month balance transfer (0% for 36 months, 3.15% fee)**
- MSE lists this as a *definite* 36 months at 0% with a 3.15% fee, meaning every accepted applicant gets the full period rather than a shorter backup deal ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026 roundup](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)).
- You must make the transfer within 60 days of opening the account to get the 0% deal ([Barclaycard balance transfer page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Why first: on £3,000 the fee is about £94.50, and 36 months means clearing it at roughly £83 a month. The "definite" length is the big plus; you know exactly what you're getting, which matters for a plan to actually clear the debt.

**2. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Mastercard (0% for up to 38 months, 3.49% fee)**
- TSB's own page says 0% for up to 38 months on balance transfers, with a 3.49% fee on transfers made in the first 90 days, and a representative APR of 24.9% (variable) afterwards ([TSB balance transfers page](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers.html)).
- MSE calls it the longest 0% deal on the market but flags that it's only "up to" 38 months, so some accepted applicants may be offered a shorter term ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)). Which? also lists it at 38 months with a 3.49% fee and 24.9% APR after ([Which? best 0% balance transfer cards](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).
- Why second: two extra months, but a higher fee (about £104.70 on £3,000) and no guarantee of the full length. Only worth picking over Barclaycard if the eligibility calculator says you'll get the full 38 months and you want the lowest possible monthly payment (about £79).

**3. Barclaycard Platinum 14-month no-fee balance transfer (0% for up to 14 months, 0% fee)**
- Barclaycard's page lists 0% on balance transfers for up to 14 months from account opening, 0% transfer fee, transfers must be made within 60 days, and 24.9% representative APR (variable) after the deal ([Barclaycard balance transfer page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). It's listed as an "up to" deal, so some applicants get less ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)); a Finder review notes a minimum income requirement of £20,000 a year ([Finder review](https://www.finder.com/uk/barclaycard-platinum-15-month-balance-transfer-visa)).
- Why third rather than first: it's the cheapest option (no fee at all), and MSE's rule of thumb is to always go no-fee if you're sure you can repay in time ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)). But clearing £3,000 in 14 months means about £215 a month, every month. If that's comfortable for you, this card moves to number one. If it isn't, paying a ~£95 fee for 36 months is the safer route; the whole point is to be certain you clear it before the interest returns.

A few things that apply to all of them: you generally can't transfer between cards from the same provider, so if your current card is a Barclaycard, options 1 and 3 are out ([MSE balance transfer guide](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum, don't spend on the new card, and ideally set a fixed payment that clears the balance before the 0% ends.

## Turn 2 (model)
Thanks: £150 to 200 a month, a fee cap of about 3%, no existing relationship with any big issuer, and a Fair rating with one missed payment 20 months ago. That rules out TSB (3.49% fee) and shifts the ranking a fair bit. At £150 to 200 a month you'll clear £3,000 in 15 to 20 months, so you don't need 36 months; what you need is a 0% period comfortably longer than 20 months, the lowest fee that buys it, and a card that's realistic for a Fair record. Here's my final top three.

**1. Barclaycard Platinum up to 22-month balance transfer (0% for up to 22 months, 1.49% fee)**
- Barclaycard's own product page lists 0% on balance transfers for up to 22 months with a 1.49% transfer fee, 24.9% representative APR (variable) after the deal, and transfers must be made within 60 days of opening ([Barclaycard Platinum up to 22 month page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/mid-bt-platinum)). The 0% clock runs from the account opening date, not the transfer date ([Barclaycard promotional rates](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/promotional-rates)).
- Why first: the fee is about £45 on £3,000, less than half what the 36-month cards charge, and 22 months still leaves a buffer over your 15 to 20 month plan. At £150 a month you'd clear it in month 20; at £200 in month 15. It's an "up to" deal, so Fair-band applicants can be offered a shorter term; run Barclaycard's eligibility checker (soft search, no mark on your file) first and only apply if it shows the full 22 months ([Barclaycard eligibility checker](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/eligibility-checker)).

**2. HSBC Balance Transfer Credit Card (0% for up to 36 months, 3.09% fee)**
- HSBC's page advertises 0% for up to 36 months on transfers made within 60 days of opening, a 3.09% fee (minimum £5) per transfer, and 25.9% representative APR afterwards ([HSBC Balance Transfer Card](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)). MSE lists the same 36 months and 3.09% fee ([MSE balance transfer guide](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Why second: it's the biggest safety net inside your 3% cap (fee about £93), and HSBC is a big issuer you have no existing card with, so no transfer-between-same-provider problem. The catch for a Fair rating: HSBC says accepted applicants may instead be offered a backup rate such as 0% for 14 months at 29.9% APR ([HSBC Balance Transfer Card](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)); 14 months would be too short for your plan. So only go ahead if the MSE eligibility calculator or HSBC's own checker shows you the full term ([MSE eligibility calculator](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).

**3. Barclaycard Platinum 36-month balance transfer (0% for 36 months, 3.15% fee, "definite")**
- MSE lists this as a definite 36 months at 0% with a 3.15% fee, meaning every accepted applicant gets the full 36 months rather than a shorter backup ([MSE, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/tips/2026/02/24/)); transfers within 60 days, 24.9% representative APR after ([Barclaycard balance transfer cards](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Why third: 3.15% is a hair over your cap (about £94.50, so roughly £1.50 more than HSBC), but the "definite" length is valuable with a Fair record; if you're accepted, there's no risk of being handed a short term. Virgin Money's 36-month card is a near-identical alternative at 3.1% fee and 24.9% APR ([Which? table](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)), but Virgin describes it as "up to" 36 months ([Virgin Money balance transfer cards](https://uk.virginmoney.com/cards/products/balance-transfer-cards/)), so it carries the same shorter-term risk as HSBC.

**How I'd play it:** run the MSE eligibility calculator once; it soft-searches and shows your likely 0% length for each card before you apply ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). If it shows the full 22 months on the Barclaycard 1.49% card, take it and set a £150 to 200 standing payment. If it shows a shorter term on that card but the full 36 on HSBC or the Barclaycard 36, go with one of those; paying £93 instead of £45 is a cheap price for certainty, and still far less than the roughly £60 a month in interest you're paying now. Apply for one card only (you can't hold both Barclaycards' deals at once, and multiple applications leave hard searches on your file), and make the transfer straight away; all three have a 60-day deadline.

A note on your credit record: comparison sites say the best deals usually need a good score and Fair applicants may be offered a higher fee or shorter term ([Forbes Advisor UK](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/uk/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)), which is exactly why checking first matters. Being on the electoral roll with an otherwise clean 20 months helps, but I can't promise acceptance; the eligibility check is the only honest way to find out.
