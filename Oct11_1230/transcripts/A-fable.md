But the fee matters on a balance this size: 3.15% is about £95, 1.25% is about £38, and no-fee is £0. Longer deals cost more up front, so you only want the long ones if you genuinely need the time.

**1. Barclaycard Platinum up to 12-month balance transfer, no fee** (best if you can pay about £250 a month)
- 0% for up to 12 months, 0% transfer fee, 24.9% rep APR afterwards, transfers must be made within 60 days of opening ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).

- Reasoning: £3,000 over 12 months is £250/month and you'd pay nothing at all. If that's realistic, this is the cheapest route. If you'd only get 6 months, drop to option 2. Santander also has a 12-month no-fee card at 24.9% afterwards if Barclaycard turns you down ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).

- Definite 36 months at 0%, meaning everyone accepted gets the full term rather than a shorter "up to" offer; 3.1% fee; 24.9% rep APR afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).

- Reasoning: this is the longest definite term with a fee right at your 3% ceiling, and you have no issuer-group conflict. MSE's rule is to pick the lowest-fee card whose 0% period is long enough to clear the balance, and "if unsure, play safe and go long" ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). So this is the better pick if you're confident in the £150+ a month; option 1 if you want to insure against a bad year. Apply for one or the other, not both.

**3. HSBC balance transfer card, 3.09% fee (36 months)**

- Reasoning: practically identical to option 1 and a sensible fallback if Virgin turns you down or the calculator shows a weak chance. I've put it third because of the discrepancy between MSE and HSBC's own page on whether the term is guaranteed and on the APR after the deal; go with HSBC's page as the final word.

**Two I considered and dropped:** Capital One's new "up to 36 months at 2.9%" (28.9% afterwards, and the term shown can be anywhere from 18 to 36 months) ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)) because Capital One's own site currently only shows an 18-month, 3%-fee card at 34.9% rep APR ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.co.uk/)), so I couldn't verify it and the higher go-to rate is a worse safety net.
