## Turn 1 (model)
Here's my take, ranked for your situation (about £3,000 at 24.9%).

**Quick context first:** the right card for £3,000 depends mostly on how fast you can repay. At 24.9% you're paying roughly £60 a month in interest right now, so almost any 0% deal beats that. But the fee matters on a balance this size: 3.15% is about £95, 1.25% is about £38, and no-fee is £0. Longer deals cost more up front, so you only want the long ones if you genuinely need the time.

**1. Barclaycard Platinum up to 12-month balance transfer, no fee** (best if you can pay about £250 a month)
- 0% for up to 12 months, 0% transfer fee, 24.9% rep APR afterwards, transfers must be made within 60 days of opening ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Which? rates it a Best Buy and Barclaycard a Recommended Provider ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).
- Catch: it's "up to" 12 months. MSE says pre-approved applicants get 12 months, others may get 12 or 6, and its eligibility calculator shows you the outcome before you apply ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Reasoning: £3,000 over 12 months is £250/month and you'd pay nothing at all. If that's realistic, this is the cheapest route. If you'd only get 6 months, drop to option 2. Santander also has a 12-month no-fee card at 24.9% afterwards if Barclaycard turns you down ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).

**2. Tesco Bank 20-month balance transfer, 1.25% fee** (best if you can pay about £150 a month)
- Definite 20 months at 0% (not "up to"), 1.25% fee, 24.9% rep APR afterwards; listed as new on MSE's low-fee table ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Reasoning: the fee is only about £38 on £3,000 and the term is guaranteed, so there's no risk of being given a shorter deal. Close alternative: Virgin Money's definite 24 months at 1.95% (about £59 fee, 24.9% afterwards) if you want the extra four months ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/), [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/clydesdale-bank-b-credit-card)).

**3. Barclaycard Platinum up to 36-month balance transfer, 3.15% fee** (best if you need the longest runway, about £85 a month)
- 0% for up to 36 months, 3.15% fee, 24.9% rep APR afterwards, transfer within 60 days ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- £20 cashback if you apply in the Barclays app, hold a Barclays current account and transfer at least £2,500 within 60 days; that offer closes at 10:00 on 20 October 2026 ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). MSE also mentions a free 12-month Apple TV offer for new customers ending 20 October ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Catch: "up to" again. MSE says non-pre-approved applicants could get 36, 32 or 18 months ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Reasoning: the fee is about £95, so I'd only pick this if £150+/month isn't doable. If you want a guaranteed 36 months, MSE lists HSBC (3.09% fee) and Virgin Money (3.1% fee) as definite 36-month cards at 24.9% afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). One warning: HSBC's own page currently says "up to 36 months", 25.9% rep APR, a £6,800 minimum income, and you can't transfer from HSBC, first direct or M&S cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)), so treat the provider page as the final word. TSB's 38-month card is the longest around but the 3.49% fee is the highest ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).

**Whatever you pick:** run MSE's eligibility calculator first so you only apply where you're likely to be accepted, make the transfer inside the 60-day window, pay at least the minimum every month, and don't spend on the new card.

## Turn 2 (model)
Thanks, that's clear: £150 to £200 a month, 18 to 24 months to clear, you'd like a longer 0% as a buffer, a fee up to about 3% is fine, no Barclays account, no existing links to the big issuers, and clean credit. On those numbers, £3,000 at £150/month takes 20 months and at £200/month takes 15, so a 24-month definite card already covers you and a 36-month definite card is a generous buffer for an extra £35 or so in fee. Here's my final three.

**1. Virgin Money 36-month balance transfer card, 3.1% fee (definite 36 months)**
- Definite 36 months at 0%, meaning everyone accepted gets the full term rather than a shorter "up to" offer; 3.1% fee; 24.9% rep APR afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Comparethemarket confirms 36 months at 3.1%, transfers must be made within the first 60 days, and it's for new customers only ([Comparethemarket](https://www.comparethemarket.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)).
- Fee on £3,000: about £93. You can't transfer from another Virgin Money or Nationwide card, which doesn't affect you since your balance is with a small online lender ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/credit-cards/)).
- Reasoning: this is the longest definite term with a fee right at your 3% ceiling, and you have no issuer-group conflict. With a clean file you should see a clear answer in MSE's eligibility calculator, which shows acceptance odds without affecting your credit score ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). One thing to double-check before applying: Virgin's generic credit cards page currently shows a 27.9% representative APR for its balance transfer range ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/credit-cards/)), so confirm the 24.9% figure on the specific card's summary box.

**2. Virgin Money 24-month balance transfer card, 1.95% fee (definite 24 months)**
- Definite 24 months at 0%, 1.95% fee, 24.9% rep APR afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)); Finder also lists this card at 24 months, 1.95% and 24.9% ([Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/clydesdale-bank-b-credit-card)).
- Fee on £3,000: about £59, so roughly £35 cheaper than option 1.
- Reasoning: 24 months comfortably covers your 15 to 20 month plan with a few months spare. MSE's rule is to pick the lowest-fee card whose 0% period is long enough to clear the balance, and "if unsure, play safe and go long" ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). So this is the better pick if you're confident in the £150+ a month; option 1 if you want to insure against a bad year. Apply for one or the other, not both.

**3. HSBC balance transfer card, 3.09% fee (36 months)**
- MSE lists it as a definite 36 months at 3.09% with 24.9% afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). HSBC's own page, however, says "up to 36 months", 25.9% rep APR, transfers within 60 days of opening, a 3.09% fee (minimum £5), minimum income of £6,800, and no transfers from HSBC, first direct or M&S cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- Fee on £3,000: about £93.
- Reasoning: practically identical to option 1 and a sensible fallback if Virgin turns you down or the calculator shows a weak chance. I've put it third because of the discrepancy between MSE and HSBC's own page on whether the term is guaranteed and on the APR after the deal; go with HSBC's page as the final word.

**Two I considered and dropped:** Capital One's new "up to 36 months at 2.9%" (28.9% afterwards, and the term shown can be anywhere from 18 to 36 months) ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)) because Capital One's own site currently only shows an 18-month, 3%-fee card at 34.9% rep APR ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.co.uk/)), so I couldn't verify it and the higher go-to rate is a worse safety net. And Tesco Bank's definite 35 months at 3.15% ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)), which is fine but slightly above your fee ceiling for one fewer month.

**Before you apply:** run MSE's eligibility calculator, apply for only one card, move the balance inside the 60-day window, set up a direct debit for a fixed £150 to 200 so the 0% isn't lost to a missed payment, and don't spend on the new card.
