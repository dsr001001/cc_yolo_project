**Provisional top three, ranked**

1. **TSB Platinum Balance Transfer: 38 months at 0%, 3.49% fee.** This is the longest interest-free window I found, which keeps the monthly payment lowest. Which? lists it at 38 months with a 3.49% fee and a 24.9% purchase rate ([Which? best 0% balance transfer cards 2026](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)). 2. **HSBC balance transfer card: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee.** Compare the Market lists this with transfers required within the first 60 days, and the offer is generally for new customers only ([Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)). The fee on £3,000 is about £93, or about £86 a month over 36 months. Check the 60-day window.

3. **Nationwide balance transfer card: 30 months at 0%, 2.99% fee (minimum £5).** This has the lowest fee of the three, about £90 on £3,000, but the shortest window, so the monthly payment is about £103 ([Nationwide announcement](https://www.nationwide.co.uk/media/news/nationwide-launches-its-longest-ever-0-percent-interest-balance-transfer-credit-card)). You need an existing Nationwide current account, savings account or mortgage ([Nationwide card page](https://www.nationwide.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-card)).

**Why this order:** the fees differ by only about £15 between the three, so the length of the 0% period matters more than the fee. A longer window means a lower monthly payment and more room for error.

**Final ranked top three**

1. **HSBC balance transfer card: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee.** Compare the Market lists 36 months at 3.09%, with transfers required within the first 60 days, and the offer is generally for new customers only ([Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)). A personal finance blog gives the fee as 3.19% and calls the 36 months "guaranteed" ([Good Money Guide](https://goodmoneyguide.com/banking/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)), so check the exact figures with HSBC. I rank it first because it has the lowest fee of the three cards that offer 36 months. On £3,000, the fee is about £93, so you would owe about £3,093. At £150 a month that clears in about 21 months, and at £200 a month in about 16 months.

2. **NatWest Longer balance transfer card: up to 36 months at 0%, 3.10% fee.** ClearScore says the 0% period could be anywhere from 16 to 36 months, depending on your credit assessment, and there is no annual fee ([ClearScore](https://www.clearscore.com/learn/credit-cards/natwest-loosens-criteria-market-leading-balance-transfer-card-heres-how-it-works)). Given your Fair band, a shorter period is a real possibility. Even the 16-month floor is workable on your budget, because clearing £3,093 in 16 months needs about £193 a month. Compare the Market also says transfers must be made within 3 months of opening the account ([Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)).

3. **Barclaycard Platinum: up to 36 months at 0%, 3.15% fee.** A MoneyComms roundup dated 29 May 2026 lists this offer ([MoneyComms](https://moneycomms.co.uk/could-a-0-balance-transfer-credit-card-work-for-you/)). I could not confirm the page for the 24.9% APR that applies afterwards, so check it on Barclaycard's site. It has the highest fee of the three and no clear advantage over HSBC or NatWest for you.

**Options I would not rank for you**

One blog says about 3%, which conflicts with Which? ([GiltEdge](https://gilt-edge.uk/posts/best-balance-transfer-cards-uk-2026-38-months-at-0-is-only-the-start)).

That means it is not a 0% deal, so I would not use it for this plan ([Vanquis](https://www.vanquis.com/credit-cards/low-apr-balance-transfer-card)).
- **MBNA:** Sources disagree on the fee, and I could not confirm the terms, so I have left it out.

**Things to know for your situation**
- Approval is not guaranteed with a Fair band score and a missed payment about 20 months ago. Use each lender's eligibility checker first. These usually use a soft search that does not appear on your credit file ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/8-things-you-need-to-know-about-balance-transfer-credit-cards-aRirT7Q1jdKn)).
- Transfer fees generally run from about 1% to 3.5% of the amount moved ([GetSmartSaver](https://getsmartsaver.co.uk/best-0-balance-transfer-cards-uk-2026/)).
- Pay more than the minimum each month, because the 0% period ends and the balance then moves to a standard rate. Also avoid new spending on the card, since purchases are often charged at a higher rate.
- The ranking is provisional. The fees and periods above come from sources of mixed date and reliability, so confirm the offer on the lender's own site before applying.
