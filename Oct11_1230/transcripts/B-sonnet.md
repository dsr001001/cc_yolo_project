## Turn 1 (model)
Here are my provisional top three. I'd like to check a few things about you before you apply (questions at the end).

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Visa: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee**
- Which? lists it at [36 months at 0%, with a 3.09% transfer fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). The card's rate after the deal is 24.9%.
- On £3,000, a 3.09% fee is about £92.70 (my arithmetic). Spread over 36 months, you'd need to pay about £86 a month to clear it.
- It has the lowest fee of the long-deal cards I found, and it's a mainstream bank. That's why it's my pick for most people.
- A different site, [MoneySuperMarket](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/news/take-control-of-your-finances-with-a-balance-transfer-credit-card), quotes a 3.19% fee and says the transfer must be made within 60 days. The two sources disagree on the fee, so check HSBC's own page before applying.

**2. Vanquis Bank Low APR Balance Transfer Visa: 36 months at 0%, 0% fee**
- Which? lists it at [36 months at 0% with no transfer fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). It was the only card in that table with no fee.
- You'd save roughly £93 compared with HSBC (my arithmetic).
- Its purchase APR is [35.9%](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards), much higher than the others. Don't use it for spending.
- Vanquis cards are generally aimed at people with weaker credit histories, but that's from my own background knowledge, not the sources above. I couldn't confirm the eligibility rules, so check them before applying. If you have a good credit score, the HSBC card is probably the easier route.

**3. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Mastercard: 38 months at 0%, 3.49% fee**
- Which? lists it at [38 months at 0% with a 3.49% fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). That's the longest 0% period in its table.
- [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards) says transfers must be made within the first 90 days. It also calls the 3.49% fee "painful".
- On £3,000 the fee is about £104.70 (my arithmetic). Choose it only if the extra two months matter to you, for example if you need to pay less than about £80 a month.

**Others I considered**
- The [MBNA Long Balance Transfer card](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/news/take-control-of-your-finances-with-a-balance-transfer-credit-card) offers 36 months at 0% with a 3.29% fee. It doesn't allow transfers from another MBNA card.
- Virgin Money and NatWest each offer [36 months with a fee of about 3.1%](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards).
- If you can clear the debt within a year, [Finder lists a Barclaycard no-fee 12-month 0% deal](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards).

**Caveats**
- The Which? table is dated 2 September 2026, and some other pages are older, so confirm the current terms on each provider's page.
- I couldn't find a MoneySavingExpert page in my searches.
- Advertised cards are only a guide. You'll be offered a card and credit limit based on your own credit check. You need a limit of at least £3,000 plus the fee for the full transfer to go through. Many providers show an eligibility checker that doesn't affect your credit score.
- Always make the minimum payment on time. Missing one can end the 0% deal.
- Set up a direct debit to clear the balance before the deal ends.
