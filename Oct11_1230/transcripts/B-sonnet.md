## Turn 1 (model)
Here are my provisional top three. I'd like to check a few things about you before you apply (questions at the end).

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Visa: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee**
- Which? lists it at [36 months at 0%, with a 3.09% transfer fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). The card's rate after the deal is 24.9%.
- On £3,000, a 3.09% fee is about £92.70 (my arithmetic). Spread over 36 months, you'd need to pay about £86 a month to clear it.
- It has the lowest fee of the long-deal cards I found, and it's a mainstream bank. That's why it's my pick for most people.
- A different site, [MoneySuperMarket](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/news/take-control-of-your-finances-with-a-balance-transfer-credit-card), quotes a 3.19% fee and says the transfer must be made within 60 days. The two sources disagree on the fee, so check HSBC's own page before applying.

**2. Vanquis Bank Low APR Balance Transfer Visa: 36 months at 0%, 0% fee**
- Which? lists it at [36 months at 0% with no transfer fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). It was the only card in that table with no fee.
- You'd save roughly £93 compared with HSBC (my arithmetic).
- Its purchase APR is [35.9%](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards), much higher than the others. Don't use it for spending.
- Vanquis cards are generally aimed at people with weaker credit histories, but that's from my own background knowledge, not the sources above. I couldn't confirm the eligibility rules, so check them before applying. If you have a good credit score, the HSBC card is probably the easier route.

**3. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Mastercard: 38 months at 0%, 3.49% fee**
- Which? lists it at [38 months at 0% with a 3.49% fee](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards). That's the longest 0% period in its table.
- [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards) says transfers must be made within the first 90 days. It also calls the 3.49% fee "painful".
- On £3,000 the fee is about £104.70 (my arithmetic). Choose it only if the extra two months matter to you, for example if you need to pay less than about £80 a month.

**Others I considered**
- The [MBNA Long Balance Transfer card](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/news/take-control-of-your-finances-with-a-balance-transfer-credit-card) offers 36 months at 0% with a 3.29% fee. It doesn't allow transfers from another MBNA card.
- Virgin Money and NatWest each offer [36 months with a fee of about 3.1%](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards).
- If you can clear the debt within a year, [Finder lists a Barclaycard no-fee 12-month 0% deal](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards).

**Caveats**
- The Which? table is dated 2 September 2026, and some other pages are older, so confirm the current terms on each provider's page.
- I couldn't find a MoneySavingExpert page in my searches.
- Advertised cards are only a guide. You'll be offered a card and credit limit based on your own credit check. You need a limit of at least £3,000 plus the fee for the full transfer to go through. Many providers show an eligibility checker that doesn't affect your credit score.
- Always make the minimum payment on time. Missing one can end the 0% deal.
- Set up a direct debit to clear the balance before the deal ends.

If you answer 1c, I'd move Vanquis up. If you answer 2c, I'd move TSB up. If you answer 2a, I'd keep HSBC first.

## Turn 2 (model)
Your answer to the first question matters: with a Fair rating, whether a card approves you, and at what limit, is as important as the headline deal. Here is my ranked top three. I checked the numbers against your £150 to £200 a month.

**1. Tesco Bank Balance Transfer card: 24 months at 0%, 2.75% fee**
- [Tesco's page](https://www.tescobank.com/creditcards/lowfee/index.html) shows 0% for 24 months from account opening and a 2.75% transfer fee. It says your credit limit and APR "may vary" depending on your circumstances.
- On £3,000 the fee is about £82.50, so you'd owe about £3,082.50 (my arithmetic). At £150 a month that clears in about 21 months. At £200 it clears in about 16 months. Either way you'd have a few months to spare inside the 24.
- It meets your fee limit and sits at the top of your 18 to 24 month range. It also isn't a high-street current-account card, so you don't need an existing relationship, though I haven't confirmed that for Tesco in particular.
- The Tesco page isn't dated, so check the terms are still live before relying on it.
- [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards) lists Tesco Bank as its pick for people with poor credit, with limits from £200. That is Finder's description, and it isn't about this specific card.
- Your limit may be lower than the £3,000 plus fee you need. If so, you could move only part of the balance.

**2. Lloyds Bank Platinum: about 23 months at 0%, 2.99% fee**
- [ClearScore](https://www.clearscore.com/learn/credit-cards/best-no-interest-purchase-credit-cards) lists up to 23 months at 0% on balance transfers with a 2.99% fee. [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/using-credit/credit-cards/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-card-deals) says the 2.99% fee applies to transfers in the first 90 days, rising to 5% after that.
- The sources disagree. An [earlier ClearScore guide](https://www.clearscore.com/learn/credit-cards/lloyds-balance-transfer-credit-card) reports 33 months at 2.49%, and says up to 49% of applicants may be offered a shorter 0% period than the headline. So you might get less than 23 months.
- At £150 to £200 a month you'd clear about £3,090 in 16 to 21 months (my arithmetic). Even a shorter offer of around 18 months would be tight but possible at the top end of your budget.
- It's second because I'm less sure of the exact terms, and your Fair rating raises the chance of a shorter offer.

**3. HSBC Balance Transfer Visa: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee**
- [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards) lists 36 months at 0% with a 3.09% fee. [MoneySuperMarket](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/news/take-control-of-your-finances-with-a-balance-transfer-credit-card) quotes a 3.19% fee and a 60-day window for the transfer.
- The fee is slightly over your "about 3%", at roughly £93 to £96 on £3,000.
- You asked for a longer 0% period as a safety net, and 36 months gives you that. You'd clear the debt in about 21 months at £150 a month, with a year to spare.
- It's third because the fee is a bit above your limit and the terms differ between sources.

**Why not the others**
- I'd leave out Vanquis for now. [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards) lists a 0% fee and 36 months, but with a 35.9% representative APR. You only need the transfer, so that matters less, but [the source](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards) only says credit is "subject to status". I couldn't find its eligibility rules. The two banks above also fit your Fair rating, and a lower-cost option is worth trying first.
- I'd leave out TSB. [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards) lists a 3.49% fee, above your limit.

**Before you apply**
- Use each bank's eligibility checker first. [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards) says TSB estimates your credit limit from a soft search, and [Ulster Bank](https://www.ulsterbank.co.uk/credit-cards/longer-balance-transfer.html) says it tells you at the start whether you'll be approved without affecting your credit score. Check whether Tesco, Lloyds and HSBC offer the same.
- Apply to one card at a time. Several hard searches in a short period can lower your score.
- Your missed payment 20 months ago may count against you, but I haven't found a source that says how much for these cards.
- Set up a direct debit for at least £150 a month so the balance is gone before the 0% ends.
- Your old card's balance is only moved when the new card is approved, so keep making its minimum payments until you see it cleared.
