## Turn 1 (model)
Hi! Good move. Interest at 24.9% on £3,000 adds up fast, and a 0% deal means every payment comes off the debt itself. I checked current offers today (11 October 2026). The deals change often, so please check the links before you apply.

**My top three for you (provisional)**

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Credit Card: best overall for most people**
- 0% on balance transfers for up to 36 months. The transfer has to be made within 60 days of opening the account ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- Transfer fee is 3.09%, minimum £5 ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)). On £3,000 that's about £92.70.
- Representative APR is 25.9% variable. Anything left after the deal moves to the standard variable rate ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)). Which? lists the APR as 24.9% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)), but HSBC's own page says 25.9%, so go with that.
- Eligibility:
  - You must be 18 or over and a UK resident, with UK taxable income or pension of at least £6,800 a year ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
  - You can't transfer from HSBC, first direct or M&S Bank cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
  - You're not eligible if you opened an HSBC credit card or got a limit increase in the last 6 months ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- HSBC may give you a shorter deal than advertised depending on your circumstances. Its FAQ mentions 14 months at 29.9% APR as one possible outcome ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- **Why it's first:** it has the lowest fee of the roughly 3-year deals I checked. 36 months means about £86 a month clears £3,000 plus the fee.

**2. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer card: longest 0% period**
- Up to 38 months at 0% on balance transfers, then 24.9% APR representative (variable) ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)).
- The 3.49% fee applies to transfers made in the first 90 days ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)). On £3,000 that's about £104.70.
- Eligibility ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)):
  - You must be over 18, a UK resident and have a regular income.
  - You can't have any recent CCJs, defaults or bankruptcies, and you must be up to date with any TSB debts.
- **Why it's second:** it costs about £12 more in fees than HSBC, but it gives you two extra months (about £82 a month). The 90-day window is also more relaxed than HSBC's 60 days. It's the better choice if a smaller monthly payment matters more than the fee.

**3. Barclaycard Platinum 12 Month Balance Transfer: no fee, if you can clear it fast**
- 12 months at 0% with no transfer fee. Representative APR is 24.9% variable. Which? rates it a Best Buy, in tables updated 1 October 2026 ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- The minimum transfer is £100, and you can transfer up to 90% of your credit limit ([Finder](https://finder.com/uk/barclaycard-platinum-15-month-balance-transfer-visa)). Barclaycard deals usually need the transfer done within a window, typically 60 days ([Finder](https://finder.com/uk/barclaycard-platinum-15-month-balance-transfer-visa)). Finder's page was last updated in April 2025, so check those details with Barclaycard.
- **Why it's third:** it costs nothing to move the balance, but you'd need about £250 a month to clear £3,000 in 12 months. If you can afford that, it's actually the cheapest option and would jump to number 1. If not, the leftover balance starts costing interest.

**Also worth a look:** Virgin Money offers up to 36 months at 0% on transfers made in the first 60 days ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/cards/products/balance-transfer-cards/)). Which? lists its fee as 3.1% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)), but Virgin's own page shows a 27.9% representative APR, higher than the others ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/cards/products/balance-transfer-cards/)). Vanquis has a 36-month deal with no fee, but its representative APR is 40.9% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)). That's expensive if anything is left when the deal ends.

**A few tips**
- Use each provider's eligibility checker before applying. It's a soft search, so it won't affect your credit score. People with a lower credit rating may be offered a shorter 0% period ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Check who issues your current card. You usually can't transfer within the same banking group, for example from one HSBC or M&S card to another ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- Keep paying your current card until the transfer shows as complete.
- Set up a fixed monthly direct debit that clears the balance before the 0% ends.
- Don't spend on the new card. Read the terms on what happens to the 0% rate if you miss a payment.
