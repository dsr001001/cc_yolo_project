## Turn 1 (model)
Here's my take, based on what the comparison sites and issuers are showing today (11 October 2026). Quick framing first: at 24.9% you're paying very roughly £60 a month in interest on £3,000, so almost any 0% deal wins; the real choice is between a small fee for a longer runway versus no fee for a shorter one. The right pick depends mostly on how much you can repay each month.

**1. Tesco Bank Low Fee Balance Transfer Credit Card: my top pick for £3,000**
- Guaranteed 20 months at 0% on balance transfers, 1.25% transfer fee, 24.9% representative APR (variable) afterwards, transfers must be made within 90 days of opening, UK residents aged 18+ subject to status ([Tesco Bank product page](https://www.tescobank.com/credit-cards/low-fee-balance-transfer-credit-card/)). MoneySavingExpert lists the same deal as "definite 20 months, 1.25% fee, 24.9% APR after" ([MSE balance transfer guide, updated 7 Oct 2026](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Reasoning: the fee is only about £37.50 on £3,000, and the 20 months is guaranteed rather than "up to", so you know exactly what you're getting. Clearing £3,000 in 20 months is about £150/month, which is a realistic target for most people. The 90-day transfer window is also more generous than Barclaycard's 60 days.

**2. Barclaycard Platinum 12 Month Balance Transfer Visa: best if you can pay around £250/month**
- Up to 12 months at 0%, 0% transfer fee, 24.9% representative APR (variable) after, transfer within 60 days, and Barclaycard warns "the balance transfer period and interest rates may differ from those shown" ([Barclaycard balance transfer cards page](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). MSE says you may get 12 or 6 months, and that being pre-approved in its eligibility calculator gets you the full 12 ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). Which? rates it a Best Buy with a 77% customer score, tables correct as of 1 October 2026 ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).
- Reasoning: genuinely free if you can clear £3,000 in 12 months (£250/month). The catch is the "up to": if you're only offered 6 months, you'd need £500/month, so check an eligibility checker first. Santander also has a 12-month no-fee card (73% Which? score) as a fallback ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)).

**3. Virgin Money 24 Month Balance Transfer Credit Card: best if you want a bit more breathing room**
- Definite 24 months at 0%, 1.95% fee, 24.9% APR after ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)); Finder also lists it at 0% for 24 months, 1.95% fee, 24.9% representative APR variable ([Finder UK](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/virgin-money/virgin-balance-transfer)). I couldn't open Virgin Money's own page (it returned an error), so confirm the transfer deadline on their site before applying.
- Reasoning: about £58.50 in fees for 24 guaranteed months, which is roughly £125/month to clear. Costs £21 more than Tesco for four extra months. Worth it only if £150/month is a stretch.

**Not in my top three, but worth knowing:** if you really need the longest runway, HSBC and Virgin Money both offer a definite 36 months at 3.09% / 3.1% fee with 24.9% after ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)), and TSB goes to up to 38 months at 3.49% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)). For £3,000 that's £90+ in fees, which I think is overkill unless you can only manage under £100/month. Also note that transfers between cards in the same banking group generally aren't allowed ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)), so check who issues your current card.

**General cautions:** Which? notes people with lower credit ratings may be offered a shorter 0% period, and that some cards give you only 60 to 90 days to complete the transfer ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)). Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum, don't spend on the new card, and diarise the end date.
