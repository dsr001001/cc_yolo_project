## Turn 1 (model)
Here are my provisional top three. They come from comparison-site data checked on 1 October 2026, not from the providers' own pages, so confirm each one with the provider before you apply.

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Credit Card (Visa)**
- It gives 36 months at 0%, with a 3.09% transfer fee and 24.9% APR afterwards, per the [Which? table](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards).
- On £3,000 the fee is about £92.70 (my arithmetic: 3.09% of £3,000).
- Clearing £3,000 in 36 months means about £83 a month.
- It is the best mix of long 0% and a lower fee than TSB.
- Virgin Money's 36-month card is almost identical, at 3.1% and 24.9% on the [same Which? page](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards). It makes a good backup if HSBC declines you.

**2. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Card (Mastercard)**
- It has the longest 0% period listed, 38 months. The fee is 3.49% and the APR afterwards is 24.9%, per [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards). [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards) also lists the 38 months.
- The fee on £3,000 is about £104.70 (my arithmetic), roughly £12 more than HSBC for two extra months.
- It's worth choosing if you need the lowest monthly payment, about £79 a month over 38 months.

**3. Barclaycard Platinum 12 Month Balance Transfer (Visa)**
- It has 12 months at 0% with no transfer fee, and 24.9% APR afterwards. Which? marks it as a Best Buy and Recommended Provider, per [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards).
- It saves you the roughly £93 fee, but you would need to pay about £250 a month to clear £3,000 in time.
- Santander's no-fee card is also 12 months, per the [same page](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards).

**Worth knowing**
- Vanquis has a 36-month, no-fee card, but its APR afterwards is 40.9% according to [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards). I left it out because of that high rate. Another summary I saw said 35.9%, so check the figure if you consider it.
- You usually can't transfer a balance between cards from the same provider, and you should check whether the new provider accepts your card type, per [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/guides/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards).
- The 0% period offered depends on your circumstances, and people with lower credit ratings may get shorter periods, per the same page. Some providers offer a soft search first, so you can check eligibility without affecting your credit file.
- Your credit limit may be less than £3,000. I couldn't verify limits.
- I couldn't find MoneySavingExpert's own ranking, so none of this reflects theirs.
