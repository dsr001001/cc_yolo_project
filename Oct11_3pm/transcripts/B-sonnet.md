Here are my provisional top three, taken from Which?'s balance transfer table. [Which? table](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards) says its figures were correct as of 1 October 2026. All three are "credit subject to status", so the 0% length you're offered can be shorter than the headline one if your credit rating is weaker.

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Visa**
- It gives 36 months at 0%, with a 3.09% transfer fee and 24.9% APR after the deal ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- On £3,000 the fee is about £92.70, by my own arithmetic from that 3.09%. Clearing the balance over 36 months would take about £83 a month.
- I rank it first because it has a long 0% period and a lower fee than TSB. Which? also shows it with a 74% customer score ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Which? notes you can't transfer from a card with the same provider, so this is no use if your current card is an HSBC one ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).

**2. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer Mastercard**
- It has the longest 0% period listed, at 38 months, with a 3.49% fee and 24.9% APR after the deal ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- The fee on £3,000 is about £104.70 (my calculation), or about £79 a month over 38 months.
- It costs roughly £12 more than HSBC in fees for two extra months. That is worth it only if you need the lower monthly payment.
- Which? gives it a 73% customer score ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).

**3. Vanquis Bank Low APR Balance Transfer Visa**
- It gives 36 months at 0% with no transfer fee, which saves you about £90 compared with HSBC. The catch is the APR after the deal, which is 40.9% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- That is much higher than your current 24.9%. It only makes sense if you're confident you'll clear the balance within the 36 months.
- Its Which? customer score is 68%, lower than the others ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- An earlier search snippet quoted a different rate (35.9%). I've used the 40.9% on the Which? page I opened, so check the provider's own page before applying.

**If you'd rather clear it within a year:** the Which? Best Buys with no transfer fee are Barclaycard Platinum 12 Month (12 months, 24.9% after), Santander Everyday (12 months) and NatWest or RBS (11 months) ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). £3,000 over 12 months is £250 a month.

**Things to check before you apply**
- Make the minimum payment every month, because missing one can end the 0% deal. This is general card practice that I haven't checked on a source page.
- Which? notes that some providers don't accept Amex transfers or require them to be made by phone ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Check the fee on the provider's own page. The Which? page gives inconsistent fee ranges, up to 5% in one place and 3.5% in another ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Use an eligibility checker before applying. Money.co.uk has one that says it won't affect your credit score ([Money.co.uk](https://www.money.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers-credit-cards)).
