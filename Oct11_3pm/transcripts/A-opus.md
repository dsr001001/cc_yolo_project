## Turn 1 (model)
You're right to move this. At 24.9%, £3,000 is costing you about £60 a month in interest (my rough sum). Any of these cards would stop that, and you'd pay a one-off fee instead.

**One thing first:** you usually can't move a balance between two cards from the same banking group. HSBC, for example, won't take balances from HSBC UK, first direct or M&S Bank cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)). So who your current card is with may rule some of these out.

## My provisional top three

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer card: 0% for up to 36 months, 3.09% fee**
- **The deal:** 0% for up to 36 months on balances moved within 60 days of opening the account. The fee is 3.09% (minimum £5), which is about £93 on £3,000. Afterwards the rate goes to the standard variable rate; the page shows 25.9% p.a. variable and a 25.9% APR representative ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- **Rules:** You need to be 18 or over, a UK resident and earn at least £6,800 a year. You can't apply if you've opened an HSBC credit card or had a limit increase in the last 6 months, or if you already have certain HSBC cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- **Eligibility check:** MoneySavingExpert lists it as a "definite" 36 months if you're accepted, and you can check your chances through its eligibility calculator ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). MSE shows 24.9% as the rate afterwards, but HSBC's own page says 25.9%, so trust HSBC's figure.
- **Why it's first:** A long 0% period that you know in advance, and the lowest fee of the cards I'd put at the top. About £84 a month (£3,000 plus the fee, over 36 months) clears it with room to spare.

**2. Tesco Bank: 0% for 20 months, 1.25% fee**
- **The deal:** 0% for a "definite" 20 months, with a 1.25% fee (about £37.50 on £3,000), then 24.9% ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- **Why it's second:** If you can pay about £152 a month, this is the cheapest way to clear the debt. It saves you about £55 in fees compared with HSBC. The catch is that whatever's left after 20 months is charged at the full rate.
- **Note:** I couldn't open Tesco's own page, so check the terms there before applying.

**3. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer: 0% for up to 38 months, 3.49% fee**
- **The deal:** 0% for up to 38 months. The 3.49% fee applies to transfers made in the first 90 days, which is about £105 on £3,000. After that the rate is 24.9% APR representative (variable) ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)).
- **Rules:** You need to be 18 or over, a UK resident, with a regular income and no recent CCJs, defaults or bankruptcies. You also need to be up to date with any money you owe TSB ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)).
- **"Up to" means not guaranteed:** You might be offered less than 38 months. MSE says TSB isn't in its calculator, but TSB's own tool shows you the deal before you apply ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- **Why it's third:** It's the longest 0% period available, so choose it if you need the lowest monthly payment (about £82). It has the highest fee of the three, though.

**Close alternatives:**
- **Virgin Money:** a "definite" 36 months with a 3.1% fee ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). Good if HSBC won't accept you or you already bank with HSBC.
- **Barclaycard no-fee card:** up to 12 months with no transfer fee ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). Only worth it if you can pay about £250 a month.

## Tips that apply to all of these
- **Check eligibility first.** Use an eligibility checker before you apply, so you don't collect rejections on your credit file ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- **Move the balance quickly.** You usually need to transfer within 60 or 90 days of opening the account to get the 0% deal and the advertised fee. The 0% period usually starts when the account opens ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- **Keep paying the old card** until the transfer has gone through.
- **Don't spend on the new card.** Divide what you owe by the number of 0% months and pay at least that each month.
- **Close or freeze the old card** if you're tempted to use it.
