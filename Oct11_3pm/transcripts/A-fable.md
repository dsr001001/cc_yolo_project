## Turn 1 (model)
Good move: at 24.9% a £3,000 balance costs you roughly £60 a month in interest alone, so shifting it to 0% means every pound you pay actually reduces the debt. I checked the current offers today (MSE's table was updated 7 October 2026, Which?'s on 1 October 2026) and opened the providers' own pages where I could. Here are my top three, ranked for your situation.

**1. HSBC Balance Transfer Credit Card: 36 months at 0%, 3.09% fee**
- 0% for up to 36 months on transfers made within 60 days of opening the account; fee 3.09% (minimum £5), which is about £93 on £3,000 ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- MSE lists it as the top "longest definite" deal, meaning every accepted applicant gets the full 36 months rather than an "up to" period ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Rate afterwards: HSBC's own page shows 25.9% representative APR (variable), while [MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/) and [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D) quote 24.9%; go with HSBC's figure as it's the source.
- Eligibility: 18+, UK resident, income of at least £6,800, not an HSBC Basic Bank Account holder, no new HSBC card or limit increase in the last 6 months, and you can't transfer from HSBC/first direct/M&S cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)).
- Why first: it's the safest option. £3,000 over 36 months is only about £86 a month, and if you can pay more you simply finish sooner. The fee is higher than my other two picks, but it buys you a long cushion. Virgin Money also does 36 months at 3.1% ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)), so that's a backup if HSBC declines you.

**2. Virgin Money 24-month balance transfer card: 24 months at 0%, 1.95% fee**
- 0% for a definite 24 months, fee 1.95% (about £59 on £3,000), 24.9% rep APR afterwards per MSE ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Caveat: I couldn't open the specific product page (it returned a 404), and Virgin Money's credit card hub currently shows a 27.9% representative APR and says you can't transfer from another Virgin Money or Nationwide card ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/credit-cards/)). So treat the 1.95%/24-month figures as MSE's reporting and confirm on Virgin's application page before applying.
- Why second: at £125 a month you'd clear £3,000 in 24 months and pay roughly £34 less in fees than HSBC. It's the balance of "long enough" and "cheap" for a debt this size, provided £125/month is comfortable.

**3. Tesco Bank Low Fee Balance Transfer card: 20 months at 0%, 1.25% fee**
- 0% for a definite 20 months, fee 1.25% (about £38 on £3,000), 24.9% rep APR afterwards ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Tesco's own balance transfer page only details its 35-month/3.15% card (transfers must be made within 90 days; UK residents 18+, subject to status) and names the Low Fee card without figures ([Tesco Bank](https://www.tescobank.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer/)), so again check the live product page for the 20-month card.
- Why third: it's the cheapest of the three, but needs £150 a month to clear in time. If you miss the deadline you're back at ~24.9%, so only pick this if that repayment is genuinely safe.

**Worth knowing**
- If you can afford £250 a month, Barclaycard's 12-month 0% card has no fee at all (24.9% APR after; transfer within 60 days), though you only get the full 12 months if pre-approved ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)). Barclays current account customers can also get £20 cashback on the 36-month card by applying in the Barclays app and transferring at least £2,500 within 60 days, but that offer ends 10:00 on 20 October 2026 ([Barclaycard](https://www.barclaycard.co.uk/personal/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)).
- Which? lists a TSB card at 38 months/3.49% ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards-and-loans/credit-cards/best-credit-card-deals/best-0-balance-transfer-credit-cards-atMFj4R64A0D)), and MSE lists it as "up to 38 months" available only through TSB's own tool ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)). I've left it out of the top three because 3.49% is the highest fee and the extra two months over HSBC don't justify it on £3,000.
- Use an eligibility checker (MSE's or the lender's) before applying: it's a soft search, and "up to" deals can give shorter periods to some applicants ([MSE](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards/)).
- Whichever you choose, make the transfer promptly (HSBC and Barclaycard: 60 days; Tesco: 90 days), set up a direct debit for at least the minimum, don't spend on the new card, and ideally pay a fixed amount that clears it before the 0% ends.
