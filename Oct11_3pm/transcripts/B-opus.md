Hi! Moving £3,000 off a 24.9% card is a good plan. At that rate the balance builds up roughly £60 of interest a month, so 0% gives you real breathing room. I checked current offers today (11 October 2026) and used the provider's own page wherever I could open one. Deals change often, so please check each link before you apply.

**My provisional top three**

**1. TSB Platinum Balance Transfer: the longest 0% period**
- Up to 38 months at 0% on balance transfers ([TSB balance transfers page](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers))
- 3.49% fee on transfers made in the first 90 days ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers)), so about £105 on £3,000
- 24.9% APR representative (variable) once the deal ends ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers))
- Eligibility: over 18, UK resident, regular income, no recent CCJs, defaults or bankruptcy, and up to date with any TSB debts ([TSB](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards/balance-transfers))
- TSB's main credit cards page says the 0% applies to transfers made within the first 90 days of opening the account ([TSB credit cards](https://www.tsb.co.uk/credit-cards))
- **Why it's first:** over 38 months, £3,000 is about £79 a month. That's the smallest monthly payment of the three, and it leaves the most slack if a month goes wrong. Its rate after the deal is also the lowest of my three.
- **Check first:** the page I read doesn't say whether you can transfer from another TSB card. If your current card is TSB's, ask them.

**2. HSBC Balance Transfer Credit Card: the lowest fee of the long deals**
- Up to 36 months at 0%, but only on transfers made within 60 days of opening ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/))
- 3.09% fee, minimum £5 ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)), so about £93 on £3,000
- 25.9% APR representative (variable) after the deal ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/)). Which? listed this card at 24.9% in a table dated 2 September ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)), so HSBC may have raised the rate since. I've used HSBC's own figure.
- Eligibility: 18+, UK resident, income of at least £6,800, and no HSBC Basic Bank Account. You also can't have opened an HSBC credit card or had a limit increase in the last 6 months ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/))
- You can't transfer from HSBC, first direct or M&S Bank cards ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.co.uk/credit-cards/products/balance-transfer/))
- **Why it's second:** it's about £12 cheaper up front than TSB, but you get 2 fewer months, about £83 a month, and a higher rate if anything is left at the end.

**3. Virgin Money Balance Transfer Card: a backup if 1 or 2 turn you down**
- Up to 36 months at 0%, on transfers made within the first 60 days ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/virgin/credit-cards/balance-transfer))
- 27.9% APR representative (variable) ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/virgin/credit-cards/balance-transfer)). That's the highest of the three if you don't clear it in time.
- **The fee wasn't shown on the page I opened** ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/virgin/credit-cards/balance-transfer)). Which? lists a Virgin Money 36-month card with a 3.1% fee ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/credit-cards/balance-transfer-credit-cards)), but I couldn't confirm that with Virgin Money.
- You can transfer up to 95% of your credit limit. You can't transfer from Nationwide or Virgin Money cards ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/virgin/credit-cards/balance-transfer)).
- The eligibility checker uses a soft search, which doesn't affect your credit score ([Virgin Money](https://uk.virginmoney.com/virgin/credit-cards/balance-transfer))
- **Why it's third:** the 0% period is good, but the fee isn't clear and the rate afterwards is the worst of the three.

**Also worth knowing:** Finder lists a Barclaycard with 12 months at 0% and no transfer fee ([Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/credit-cards/best-credit-cards)). I didn't confirm this with Barclaycard. It only makes sense if you could pay about £250 a month.

**General tips**
- Run each provider's eligibility checker before applying. Most use a soft search, so they don't affect your credit score.
- You may get a lower credit limit than £3,000. In that case you can transfer part of the balance and pay down the rest.
- Make the transfer within the deadline: 60 days for HSBC and Virgin Money, 90 days for TSB.
- Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum payment every month.
- Don't spend on the new card.
